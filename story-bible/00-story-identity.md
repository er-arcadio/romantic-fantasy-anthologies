# Story Identity

## Premise
Adult romantic fantasy set on prehistoric Earth, in the age of Pangea, among iconic fantasy archetypes: witches, hags, fairies, elves, dark elves, drow, vampires, werewolves, demons, fallen angels, and a death god. Told across seven eras and an epilogue, out of chronological order.

## Core principles
1. **Women lead.** Every story's protagonist is a woman; her goals, choices, and growth drive the plot.
2. **Men support.** Men have real inner lives, but their arcs serve, complicate, or deepen hers. This deliberately flips the usual trope of male protagonists with women in support.
3. **The gazes are crossed.** Women are written through the male gaze, men through the female gaze (`../writing-guide/gaze-guide.md`).
4. **Archetype filter.** Every creature has a strong, recognizable visual identity that artists have long depicted, and a clear driving mission in its lore. Pure cautionary-tale spirits were cut for failing this test.
5. **A few drive the plot; most seek peace and love.** Good and evil are driven by a select few who create havoc or stand against it. The vast majority of people just want peace, love, and sex, and everyday life should show it.
6. **Adults only in adult content.** `../writing-guide/story-laws.md` → 1. Absolute.

## The thesis
> **"The meaning of life is purpose, community, and good health. Everything else, you will find, doesn't matter."**

The stories **pressure-test** this claim rather than endorse it. Each story strains it from at least one angle: **political structures** (councils, courts, who rules magic), **religious structures** (heaven, the underworld, doctrine), or **tradition** ("because it's always been that way"). The thesis can bend, narrow, or be partly contradicted. That is the point. The verdict for each group at the end of the saga is in `eras/era-7-witches.md` and `../themes/thesis.md`.

## Conflict model
("Man" here means *person*.)
- **Person vs self:** drive and self tend to win, but not always tragically. Self-victory can be liberating, costly, or bittersweet.
- **Person vs society:** the person tends to win.

## Themes, in rough priority
1. **Sex**, as an adult subject, not a garnish
2. **Philosophy of living**, deliberately counter to mainstream Western norms
3. **Morals and the moral compass**: what it is, where it comes from, when it fails
4. **Mental health**: apathy, shame, hunger, grief, treated honestly

## Underlying theme
Extremes versus the middle path: is balance reached through drastic action, or by small adjustments, "a little more left, a little more right"?
