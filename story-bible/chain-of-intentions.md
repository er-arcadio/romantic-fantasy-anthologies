# The Chain of Intentions

The causal spine of the whole world. Every era must stay consistent with it (`../writing-guide/story-laws.md` → 5).

1. **Humans are progressing, and the gods supervise.**
2. **First generation, the fairies:** push toward more progress, with hints only. Not enough.
3. **Second generation, the elves:** pull toward even more progress, openly. Some elves pull against it, but not strongly enough.
4. **The fallen angels** come to restore equilibrium by pulling toward **less progress**: humans' vices and hedonism. Their justification is that humans are over-fixed on progress.
5. **Two views of how to restore balance:**
   - **The leader (a woman fallen angel):** balance by **swaying the living**, a little more left and a little more right, **never by killing**.
   - **The man who becomes the death god:** has **always believed in neutrality**, even as an angel. His flaw is the method: he deludes himself that **killing all magical beings is the only way to true neutrality**. He never learns otherwise; he only changes tactics.
6. **The witches** share the leader's goal, balance without killing, **except the evil witch**, who sides with the death god.
7. **Containment, then the demons.** The first witch contains the death god in the underworld, then uses him to make the **demons**, male beings meant to seduce the progressive elves. A coven sister seduced by the death god helps, secretly hoping to win his heart. She becomes the evil witch.
8. **The demons miss their target.** Built for the progressive elves, they also seduce the dark elves, many witches, and the fairies.
9. **The vampires.** After the evil witch's defeat, the death god decides his methods must be **more infectious and sly**. The vampire is his first move: made for sheer killing, aimed at witches and elves, though it also kills and recruits humans. Vampires can kill witches, elves, and some demons, but not a fairy in her full form; only in her magic-less human form.
10. **Later turns** (eras 5 to 7) follow the same logic: each side's attempt to tip the balance produces the next imbalance. See `timeline.md`.
