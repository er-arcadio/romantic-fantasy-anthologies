# Reproduction

Every scene of birth or conjuring depicts only the **adult** parent. Offspring are never sexualized (`../writing-guide/story-laws.md` → 1).

- **Fairies** are **conjured, not born**. A fairy conjures another by **immersing herself in the element she came from or dedicated herself to**. The new fairy is an **adult from the start**, a **mental clone** of the one who conjured her, with a different appearance (skin, hair, eye colour). Fairies can have sex with other beings but **can never conceive** with them: the parts exist but do not work. Soul fairies rarely conjure, out of shame at immersing themselves in birth or death.
- **Elves** reproduce by **parthenogenesis with sunlight and love**, usually with another elf (often sexually, but not biologically). Later they can also reproduce with a human or another creature. Their children follow the aging chart.
- **No male elves or fairies exist**, for a biological reason: parthenogenesis produces only women. A male elf needs a human father, which is why male elves first appear with the epilogue's half-births.
- **Demons** carry a curse: a demon's child with a **mortal** is always **male**, so the seduction spreads.
- **Drow** are born of demons and elves. They can be **male or female**: women lean elf with slight demon traits, men lean demon with slight elf traits.
- **Vampires** are made, not born: a vampire turns a human.
- **Werewolves** are made by a bite. **They never turn women.**
- **Witches** are made, first by the leader and then through the avenue the second angel woman opened.

## Why elves began reproducing with others
Elves and fairies came to Earth with no thought of reproducing with humans, though humans were seduced by them and a few taboo romances happened. The fallen angels brought the idea to life, raising sexual tension and disrupting the norm. At the climax of everyone trying to keep order, the elves were forced to reproduce with others to keep the balance.
