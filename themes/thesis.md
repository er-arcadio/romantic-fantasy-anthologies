# The Thesis

> **"The meaning of life is purpose, community, and good health. Everything else, you will find, doesn't matter."**

The saga **pressure-tests** the claim; it does not preach it. Pressure comes from political structures, religious structures, and tradition. Stories may bend, narrow, or partly contradict it.

## Where each people ends up (Era 7)

| People | Purpose | Community | Health | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Witches | yes | yes | yes | Fulfilled |
| Forest fairies | yes | yes | yes | Fulfilled |
| Soul fairies | yes | yes | fragile | Fulfilled, precariously |
| The death god | yes, the strongest | yes (demons, drow) | yes | Fulfilled, uncomfortably on purpose |
| Vampires | at war with identity | yes | sustained by hunger | Tragedy of identity |
| Werewolves | **no** (shame) | yes | yes | Tragedy |
| Fallen angels | yes (longing) | **no** | yes | Tragedy |
| Elves | open | yes | yes | Open |

The pattern: those who found peace mostly did it by **separating**; those still missing something either **caused the most harm** or **couldn't let go**. The tragedies are set against the fulfilled on purpose.

The death god's fulfilment is the test's sharpest edge: a purpose the reader rejects can still satisfy the thesis. The story should let that stand rather than explain it away.
