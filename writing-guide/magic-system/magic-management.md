# Magic Management

> **Guideline, not canon.** This is a general worldbuilding tool imported from the author's *Magic Management* notes. It makes no claims about this world. Any choice it suggests becomes canon only after it is filled in for this world (see `world-form.md`), rationalized, and confirmed by the author.

Why a magic system needs limits, and how a world gets from scrambling to deal with magic to some kind of stability. Set the fixed rules of the magic, choose how the shifting parts of the story bend, and follow the result through five stages: **Arrival → Scramble → Sorting → Settlement → Stability**.

## 1. Three categories of restriction

Every limitation sorts into one of three places: inside the caster (will, belief, price), outside the caster (other people, the economy, the source itself), or in the mechanism (hard facts about how the magic works). One restriction from each usually carries a story further than five from the same place.

### Internal: What the caster won't do
*Restricts from inside the caster*

- **Moral / ethical.** Capability exceeds willingness. The terrible, efficient option is right there and the caster doesn't take it, or does, and that's the tragedy. *In the explorer: the ethic turning point.*
- **Psychological / manipulation.** Capability exceeds belief. Someone is powerful but has been convinced they aren't, or that the price is worse than it is. *In the explorer: the belief turning point.*
- **Cost / consequence.** Magic works, but it charges rent: life, memory, sanity, years. The question stops being "can I" and becomes "will I pay." *In the explorer: the Cost rule, plus the price turning point.*

### External: What others won't allow
*Restricts from outside the caster*

- **Legal / institutional.** Capability exceeds permission. Anyone could; a state, guild, or church forbids it, often one branch at a time. Corruption and black markets live here. *Rules: Access, Branches.*
- **Resource / access.** Capability exceeds supply. Power is gated by stones, wells, or ore, so magic becomes an economy with owners. *Rules: Where power lives, Population.*
- **Source-nature.** The source has terms of its own: a will, a supply that's running out, a pact it can revoke. It acts like a character, not a fuel tank. *Rule: Supply.*

### Structural: What the magic itself won't do
*Hard facts about the mechanism*

- **Mastery / specialization.** Getting good takes practice. What matters is whether practice evens people out or pulls a few far ahead, and whether some branches, like time, take far more than others. *Rules: Skill spread, Reach, Branches.*
- **Scale.** Whether one person can only do what a person with tools could already do, or can break the world: alter reality, open portals, bend time. *Rule: Scale.*
- **Biological / bodily.** The body is the bottleneck: burnout, physical toll, and marks that make power visible and therefore huntable. *Rule: Visibility.*
- **Knowledge / awakening.** Whether untrained power leaks out on its own, can be found alone, or has to be taught. *Rule: Awakening.*
- **Transfer.** Whether power can be taken from someone. Inheritance is a creative layer you can add to any power that lives in the person. *Rule: Can it be taken.*

## 2. The rules (fixed when magic arrives)

Rules decide what can happen. States (section 3) shift over the story at turning points.

### The source

**Where power lives** (can it be owned)
- *Ownable*: In a thing or a place: stones, a well, a crater, a region. It can be seized, fenced, and sold. Ambient magic that's stronger in some places works like this.
- *In the person*: Carried by the caster. You can't take the magic without taking the person. Ambient magic that's the same everywhere works like this.

**Supply** (does the source change over time)
- *Steady*: The total never changes. Nothing is running out.
- *Running out*: There's a fixed amount, and every use spends some.
- *Has a will*: The source chooses, bargains, or revokes. It's a player, not a resource.

### Who has it

**Access** (who can use it at all)
- *Some people*: Only some people can use it.
- *Everyone*: Anyone can, in theory.

**Population** (how many, if only some)
- *1*: One person, ever.
- *2–100*: A group you could name.
- *200+*: Too many to know by name.
- *Everyone*: Set by Access.

**Visibility** (can you tell who has it)
- *Hidden*: Casters look like anyone else.
- *Marked*: It shows in the eyes, the skin, a glow, or a scar.

**Can it be taken** (can power move between people)
- *No*: Your power is yours. Killing you doesn't give it to anyone.
- *Yes*: Power can be given, sold, or stolen, and stacked.

### What it can do

**Scale** (how world-breaking one person can be)
- *Human-scale*: Does what people could already do with tools and weapons: heal, burn, build, kill.
- *World-breaking*: Does what nothing could before: alter reality, open portals, bend time.

**Reach** (can a caster do whatever they want)
- *Bounded*: You know what a caster can do: a single gift, or a reserve that runs dry.
- *Open*: A skilled caster can do anything, again and again.

**Branches** (are some kinds far harder)
- *Flat*: Every kind of magic is about as hard as any other.
- *Tiered*: A few branches, like time, minds, or death, are far rarer and harder.

**Cost** (does using it take something)
- *Free*: Nothing beyond effort.
- *Priced*: It takes something: life, memory, sanity, years, or something from the world.

### How people get good

**Awakening** (what happens before you're trained)
- *Leaks out*: Untrained power fires by reflex: accidents, misfires, panic.
- *Found by practice*: Nothing happens until you try, but anyone can work it out alone.
- *Must be taught*: Nothing happens until someone who knows shows you how.

**Skill spread** (what practice does to the gap)
- *Evens out*: Practice brings most people to about the same level.
- *Stratifies*: Practice pulls a dedicated few far ahead of everyone else.

## 3. States and turning points (shift over the story)

**Price turning point**
- *Made public*: The price becomes common knowledge, and restraint starts to look rational.
- *Buried*: Whoever connects the pattern first keeps it quiet. Everyone else keeps paying.

**Belief turning point**
- *Corrected*
- *Kept*

**Ethic turning point**
- *Restraint wins*: Casual use becomes shameful. Norms do the work laws would otherwise do.
- *Pragmatism wins*: It's a tool like any other: regulate it, insure it, use it.
- *Might wins*: If you can, you should. Power sorts by force.

- **Price turning point:** using magic has been costing people something, and someone finally connects the pattern. It surfaces in the Scramble if untrained power leaks or everyone has access, otherwise in Sorting. A free magic has no price turning point.
- **Belief turning point** (always in Sorting): if casters started out *feared* (power leaks or casters are marked), the fear gets tested; *corrected* means the public learns what casters can and can't do, *kept alive* means someone benefits from the fear. If they started out *underestimated*, someone finds out they're stronger than they were told; *corrected* spreads the truth, *managed* keeps everyone convinced they're weak.
- **Ethic turning point:** in the Scramble if power leaks or is world-breaking ("the first real damage"), otherwise in Sorting ("the first magical crime"). The public argues about what magic is for.

## 4. Stage 1, Arrival: how it starts

Each onset is weighted by the rules that make it likely (weight 3 = strong).

### Unnoticed Emergence
Nothing happens unless someone knows what to do, so for months or years there's no public event. A few people find it in private. When it surfaces, it shows up first as rumor, then as a viral clip, then as a cover-up.

- (3) It has to be taught, and nobody knows how to teach it yet. *[Awakening: Must be taught]*
- (2) It only shows when someone practices, and early practitioners can hide it. *[Awakening: Found by practice; Visibility: Hidden]*
- (1) There are only a few people who have it, and they can hide it. *[Population: 1 or 2–100; Visibility: Hidden]*

### Uncontrolled Release
Magic fires by reflex and people don't know how to hold it back. The first weeks look like a wave of unexplained accidents: fires, collapses, panic deaths. People learn what it is by watching someone fail to control it.

- (3) It fires on its own, and nobody knows how to hold it back. *[Awakening: Leaks out]*
- (1) Everyone is an untrained beginner at the same moment. *[Awakening: Leaks out; Access: Everyone]*
- (1) When a misfire can tear reality, accidents become disasters. *[Awakening: Leaks out; Scale: World-breaking]*

### Gold Rush
Power lives in a place or an object, so everyone's first move is to reach it. Sites get swarmed, fenced off, or seized by whoever has the most guns nearby.

- (3) Power lives in a place or object, and whoever gets there first gets it. *[Where power lives: Ownable]*
- (1) It's running out, so getting there first matters more. *[Where power lives: Ownable; Supply: Running out]*

### Witch Hunt
The few who have it are visibly marked. The public knows who they are before anyone knows what they can do, and fear fills the gap.

- (3) People can see who has it before anyone knows what they can do. *[Visibility: Marked; Access: Some people]*

### Global Shock
Everyone gets it at once and nobody needs a teacher. There's no discovery phase. Within weeks, armies, police, and insurers are matched by everyone they used to control.

- (3) Everyone has it at once, and it only takes practice. *[Access: Everyone; Awakening: Found by practice]*
- (2) Everyone has it at once, and it's already firing. *[Access: Everyone; Awakening: Leaks out]*
- (1) Everyone's potential is world-breaking. *[Access: Everyone; Scale: World-breaking]*

### Quiet Recruitment
The few who have it can hide it. Whoever finds them first, whether a government, a corporation, or a cartel, recruits them quietly. The public doesn't find out until something leaks.

- (3) A hidden few, found by the powerful first. *[Access: Some people; Visibility: Hidden; Population: 1 or 2–100; Awakening: Leaks out or Found by practice]*

### First Contact
The source announces itself, or starts choosing people. Discovery looks less like a science story and more like a revelation.

- (3) The source has a will, and it acts first. *[Supply: Has a will]*

## 5. Stage 2, Scramble: who moves first

Typical first moves, depending on the rules: states and companies seize ownable sites; emergency powers when power leaks; marked casters listed before anyone knows what they can do; a willful source making its first choices; the few who can teach becoming the most sought-after people alive; governments hunting world-breakers; everyone trying at once when access is universal; the first killings for power when power can be taken; someone measuring a finite supply; the world trying to work out who the one caster is. If none apply, things move slowly and institutions have time to notice.

## 6. Stage 3, Sorting: who rises

- **Skill spread evens out:** numbers and organization matter more than any single caster. **Stratifies:** most stay dabblers; a dedicated few become vastly stronger, with blind spots. With a single caster, everyone ranks by their relationship to that person.
- Bounded reach sorts people by *what* they can do as much as how well; open reach makes choosing a specialty a life decision.
- Tiered branches produce the rarest masters, whom every institution wants to recruit or ban.
- Taught magic makes a good teacher matter as much as effort.
- Takeable power lets people rise by taking, not only earning.
- At the top of a world-breaking, stratified curve, one master can outweigh an army.

## 7. Stage 4, Settlement: what order forms

Each response is weighted by the rules and states that lead to it (weight 3 = strong fit). If two strong responses would fight each other, that conflict is probably the plot.

### The state

**State Control**: Whoever runs security moves first. With a small, listable population, this looks like registration and licensing. With everyone holding world-breaking power, it looks like curfews and martial law. The texture differs, but the same actor gets there first.
- (3) Everyone holding world-breaking power is an emergency, and the state answers with force first. *[Access: Everyone; Scale: World-breaking]*
- (2) A group big enough to matter and small enough to list is the easiest thing for a state to register. *[Access: Some people; Population: 2–100 or 200+]*
- (2) Untrained power misfiring in public invites emergency powers. *[Awakening: Leaks out]*
- (1) Marked casters are easy to find, which makes a registry enforceable. *[Visibility: Marked; Access: Some people]*
- (1) A group of people who can break the world is a national-security problem. *[Scale: World-breaking; Access: Some people; Population: 2–100 or 200+]*

**Branch Prohibition**: Laws against specific kinds, not magic itself. Everyone may use magic, but necromancy, time, and minds are illegal. The law draws lines between branches. Enforcement is uneven and exceptions get bought, so corruption follows.
- (3) When a few branches, like time, minds, or death, are rarer and more dangerous, the law targets those instead of magic as a whole. *[Branches: Tiered]*
- (1) When everyone can try, banning the worst branches is easier to enforce than banning magic. *[Branches: Tiered; Access: Everyone]*
- (1) Once the price is public, the costliest branches get banned first. *[Cost: Priced; Price turning point: Made public; Branches: Tiered]*
- (1) If a skilled caster can do anything, the law has to name what they may not do. *[Reach: Open; Scale: World-breaking]*

**Managed Myth**: Order through what people believe they can't do. The ceiling is a story. Whoever benefits keeps people convinced they're weaker than they are, or keeps the price quiet. The arc writes itself when someone finds out the limit was never real.
- (3) People started out thinking magic was small or fake, and whoever benefits keeps it that way. This is the "you're not all-powerful" story. *[Belief turning point: Kept; Awakening: Found by practice or Must be taught; Visibility: Hidden]*
- (3) The few who know the price keep it quiet. That's power, whether they're protecting people or profiting from them. *[Cost: Priced; Price turning point: Buried]*
- (1) Whoever controls teaching controls what people think is possible. *[Awakening: Must be taught; Belief turning point: Kept]*

**Persecution**: Fear finds the visible few. Casters get registered, segregated, or hunted, and the fear doesn't have to be fair. The story engines are hiding, passing as ordinary, underground railroads, and the moment the persecuted decide to fight back.
- (3) A visible, powerful minority is the classic target of fear. *[Visibility: Marked; Access: Some people]*
- (2) The early accidents fixed an image of casters as dangerous, and nobody corrected it. *[Belief turning point: Kept; Awakening: Leaks out; Access: Some people]*
- (1) Fear of the marked never got corrected. *[Belief turning point: Kept; Visibility: Marked]*
- (1) If power can be taken, casters are prey as well as suspects. *[Can it be taken: Yes; Access: Some people]*

### Institutions & faith

**Institutional Capture**: Credentials become the gate. Universities and research bodies own the study of magic. It becomes an elite, credentialed pursuit, and self-taught casters are treated as illegitimate no matter how good they are.
- (3) When it must be taught and only some can learn, whoever holds the teachers holds the gate. *[Awakening: Must be taught; Access: Some people]*
- (1) A site to study and a classroom-sized population fit a university model. *[Where power lives: Ownable; Access: Some people; Population: 2–100]*
- (1) Years of study favor institutions that can pay for years of study. *[Skill spread: Stratifies; Access: Some people]*

**Public Schooling**: Train everyone so nobody gets hurt. Magic education becomes universal, like literacy or driver's ed. The chaos fades as a generation learns control. The fight moves to the curriculum: who decides which branches get taught, and to whom.
- (3) Untrained power hurts people, so teaching control becomes a public-safety measure. *[Access: Everyone; Awakening: Leaks out]*
- (3) It has to be taught and anyone can learn, so someone has to teach everyone. *[Access: Everyone; Awakening: Must be taught]*
- (2) Practice brings almost anyone to competence, so mass education pays off. *[Access: Everyone; Skill spread: Evens out]*
- (1) When a skilled caster can do anything, what gets taught becomes a political question. *[Access: Everyone; Reach: Open]*

**Religious Reframing**: Doctrine arrives before law. Rare, unearned, or willful power reads as blessing or curse long before it reads as policy. Casters become prophets, saints, or heretics depending on who's doing the interpreting.
- (2) One person is easier to mythologize than to legislate. *[Population: 1]*
- (2) A single figure who can break the world invites myth before it invites law. *[Population: 1; Scale: World-breaking]*
- (2) To most people, a source with a will looks like a god. *[Supply: Has a will]*
- (1) An unearned gift that shows up on its own reads as a blessing or a curse. *[Where power lives: In the person; Access: Some people; Awakening: Leaks out]*

**Pact Keepers**: Whoever speaks for the source rules. The source has terms, so someone has to negotiate them. Intermediaries such as priests, diplomats, or chosen families gain power by claiming to know what the source wants, whether or not they do.
- (3) A source with its own terms needs negotiators, and whoever speaks for it gains power. *[Supply: Has a will]*
- (1) If the source picks who gets access, its intermediaries decide who rises. *[Supply: Has a will; Access: Some people]*

### The market

**Corporate Enclosure**: Magic becomes property. Someone owns the source and licenses access. Magic becomes property, and a skilled caster with no money is locked out as surely as an unskilled one.
- (3) Something extractable and ownable is a market before it's a policy. *[Where power lives: Ownable; Access: Some people]*
- (2) If power can be moved, it can be bought and sold. *[Can it be taken: Yes]*
- (1) A place or object can be fenced, mined, and patented. *[Where power lives: Ownable]*
- (1) A shrinking supply raises the price of what's left. *[Where power lives: Ownable; Supply: Running out]*

**Black Market**: Everything banned gets sold anyway. Forbidden branches, smuggled stones, and stolen power trade in the dark. Wherever law or scarcity draws a line, a shadow economy runs along it, and so does the corruption that protects it.
- (3) Transferable power creates a trade in taking it from people. *[Can it be taken: Yes]*
- (2) Banned branches get practiced, taught, and sold in the dark. *[Branches: Tiered]*
- (1) Any licensed supply leaks. *[Where power lives: Ownable; Access: Some people]*
- (1) Rationing breeds smuggling. *[Supply: Running out]*

**Scarcity Wars**: A shrinking supply makes every holder a target. The source is running out, so the fight shifts from who may use magic to who gets the last of it. Rationing, sieges, and alliances form around whatever supply is left.
- (3) A shrinking supply turns every holder into a target. *[Supply: Running out]*
- (1) A fixed site can be besieged. *[Supply: Running out; Where power lives: Ownable]*
- (1) Sites that grant world-breaking power are strategic assets worth a war. *[Where power lives: Ownable; Scale: World-breaking]*

### Casters themselves

**Guild & Self-Regulation**: Casters make their own rules first. Casters set internal rules before any outside institution catches up. That means a formal guild when the group is tight-knit, or reputation and shunning when powers are modest and widespread.
- (3) Big enough to organize, small enough that no outside authority notices in time. *[Population: 2–100]*
- (2) When powers are modest and predictable, communities can police themselves instead of calling in the state. *[Access: Everyone; Scale: Human-scale; Reach: Bounded]*
- (1) Master-and-apprentice teaching produces guilds on its own. *[Awakening: Must be taught; Access: Some people]*
- (1) Shared norms of restraint make self-policing work. *[Ethic turning point: Restraint wins]*

**Priesthood of Mastery**: Skill, not access, decides who leads. Everyone can try, but a dedicated few get vastly better. They become the new authority, and power shifts from whoever held office before to whoever put in the years.
- (3) Anyone can try, a dedicated few get vastly better, and those few become the authority. *[Skill spread: Stratifies; Access: Everyone]*
- (2) When mastery unlocks anything, the masters are the power. *[Skill spread: Stratifies; Reach: Open]*
- (1) Masters of a world-breaking power outrank any office. *[Skill spread: Stratifies; Scale: World-breaking]*

**Gift Castes**: People become their gift. Each caster can do one predictable thing, so society sorts people by what they can do: healers, burners, finders. Gifts become professions, then castes. If gifts run in families, the castes turn hereditary. That's a creative layer you can add.
- (3) Hundreds of people, each with one predictable gift, slot into roles the way professions do. *[Reach: Bounded; Access: Some people; Population: 200+]*
- (2) When everyone has one fixed gift, the gift you're born with decides your work. *[Reach: Bounded; Access: Everyone]*
- (1) Even a small group sorts itself by gift. *[Reach: Bounded; Access: Some people; Population: 2–100]*
- (1) A gift that lives in the person follows them for life. *[Reach: Bounded; Where power lives: In the person]*

**Fragmentation**: No center holds. No central authority reasserts control in time. Society splits into zones, each run by whoever's local casters are strongest. There is still order, but it only reaches as far as one warlord's power.
- (3) World-breaking, open-ended power in everyone's hands leaves no center able to hold the map. *[Access: Everyone; Scale: World-breaking; Reach: Open]*
- (2) After the turning point, the argument that won was "if you can, you should." Power sorts by force. *[Ethic turning point: Might wins]*
- (1) Too much power, too widely held. *[Access: Everyone; Scale: World-breaking]*
- (1) If power can be stacked by taking it, warlords grow by conquest. *[Can it be taken: Yes; Scale: World-breaking]*

### Settlement & balance

**Absorption**: Existing law stretches to cover it. Magic does what people could already do with tools, so the old systems adapt. Healing gets medical licensing, fire gets weapons law, and life goes on. The drama is smaller and more personal: the first malpractice case, the first magical murder trial.
- (3) Powers are human-scale and predictable, so existing licensing and weapons law can cover them. *[Scale: Human-scale; Reach: Bounded]*
- (1) Nothing misfires on its own, so the law has time to catch up. *[Scale: Human-scale; Awakening: Found by practice or Must be taught]*
- (1) A pragmatic public treats it as one more tool to regulate. *[Scale: Human-scale; Ethic turning point: Pragmatism wins]*

**Deterrence**: A few world-breakers held in balance. A small group can do what nothing could before. Nobody can police them, so they're kept in check by each other: alliances, hostages, mutually assured destruction. Stability holds as long as none of them thinks they can win.
- (3) A small group holding world-breaking power can't be policed, only balanced. *[Scale: World-breaking; Access: Some people; Population: 2–100]*
- (1) When each world-breaker's power is known, the sides can plan around each other. *[Scale: World-breaking; Reach: Bounded]*
- (1) Whoever holds the sites holds the deterrent. *[Where power lives: Ownable; Scale: World-breaking]*

**Treaty & Refounding**: Rules written after the damage. Once the dust settles, exhausted survivors negotiate new terms, like a constitutional convention for magic. The rules come from what it already cost, not from foresight.
- (2) A change this large forces rules to be negotiated after the fact, not planned in advance. *[Access: Everyone]*
- (1) Enough people are affected that any lasting order has to be agreed, not declared. *[Population: 200+]*
- (1) The damage from untrained use is the kind that produces treaties. *[Awakening: Leaks out; Access: Everyone]*
- (1) Shock after a world-breaking catastrophe is when treaties get signed. *[Ethic turning point: Restraint wins; Scale: World-breaking]*

**Restraint Culture**: People choose not to. Most people could, but they don't, because it's seen as wrong or because the price is known. Norms do the work laws would otherwise do. The story lives in whoever decides the rule doesn't apply to them.
- (3) After the turning point, most people decided casual use is wrong. Norms now do the work laws would otherwise have to. *[Ethic turning point: Restraint wins]*
- (2) A known price makes restraint rational, not just moral. *[Cost: Priced; Price turning point: Made public]*
- (1) The bigger the spell, the bigger the bill, so the biggest powers go unused. *[Cost: Priced; Price turning point: Made public; Scale: World-breaking]*

## 8. Stage 5, Stability: what holds, and what could break it

Stability is the strongest one or two settlement responses together; with none strong, it is a patchwork of local arrangements. **Fault lines** are where a sequel starts:
- **The secret of the price** (priced and buried): when it gets out, the settlement loses legitimacy overnight.
- **Fear** (feared belief kept alive): a generation raised under suspicion pushes back. **The myth** (underestimated belief managed): one person who learns they're stronger can break the order.
- **The supply** (running out): every peace is a countdown.
- **The source's terms** (has a will): it can change its mind.
- **Stacking** (takeable): someone quietly takes power until they outweigh the settlement.
- **The next master** (stratified) who won't join the institution that trained them.
- **One miscalculation** between a few world-breakers.
- **The forbidden branches** (tiered): someone always practices them.
- **No shared ethic** (might wins): the peace is only as stable as the balance of force.

If there are no structural fault lines, the stability is real, and conflict must come from individual characters: the moral and psychological restrictions in section 1.

## 9. Where each idea lives

| Question | Where it lives | Leads to |
|---|---|---|
| Is there chaos because people release magic uncontrollably? | Awakening (Leaks out), Access | Uncontrolled Release, Global Shock → Public Schooling, Treaty |
| Maybe no one realizes yet, because it takes skill | Awakening (Found by practice, Must be taught) | Unnoticed Emergence, Quiet Recruitment → Institutional Capture |
| Do only some have access? How many? | Access, Population | State Control, Guild, Deterrence, Religious Reframing |
| Where does the magic come from? (well, stones, blood, meteor in orbit) | Where power lives, Supply | Gold Rush, First Contact → Corporate Enclosure, Pact Keepers, Scarcity Wars |
| Are some kinds harder to get, like time travel? | Branches | Branch Prohibition, Black Market |
| Is magic a skill or inherited? | Skill spread, Awakening; inheritance is a creative layer on "In the person" | Priesthood of Mastery, Gift Castes |
| How world-breaking is one person? Can they do whatever they want? | Scale, Reach | Absorption at human scale; Deterrence or Fragmentation when world-breaking |
| Good vs. evil: unlimited power doesn't mean using it is right | Ethic turning point (the world); moral restriction (each character) | Restraint Culture, or Fragmentation if might wins |
| Competition, dedication, education: dabblers vs. masters | Skill spread, Reach, Awakening | Stage 3 sorting; Public Schooling, Institutional Capture, Priesthood of Mastery |
| Judicial system and corruption: laws against specific kinds | Branches, Access, Visibility | State Control, Branch Prohibition, Black Market, Persecution |
| Capitalism: stones, mana, infinity-stone-style sources | Where power lives (Ownable), Supply, Can it be taken | Gold Rush → Corporate Enclosure, Scarcity Wars, Black Market |
| Psychological warfare: "you're not all-powerful" | Belief turning point (Managed), price turning point (Buried) | Managed Myth, and a fault line in Stability |
| A hidden price nobody's noticed yet | Cost (Priced), plus the price turning point | Restraint Culture if made public, Managed Myth if buried |
| Chaos, then rules, then schooling, then stability | Everything | The five stages: Arrival → Scramble → Sorting → Settlement → Stability |
