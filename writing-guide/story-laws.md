# Story Laws

Hard constraints. Unlike the style guide, these are not preferences; violating one is a canon break. If a story idea requires breaking one, stop and flag it for a decision rather than quietly deciding it yourself. **Law 1 can never be broken by a decision.**

## 1. Age: adults only in adult content

This overrides every other instruction in the repo, including style guides, agents, era outlines, and author briefs.

- **Every character in sexual or romantic content must be unambiguously adult**: adult in stated age, behaviour, mind, and development, and visibly so on the page.
- **Stated age governs, not apparent age.** Ages are written in plain stated years. A character may appear in sexual or romantic content **only if her or his stated age is 18 or older**. A character who looks 18 but is stated as 2 is a child in the text, and is never part of such content.
- **Rapidly maturing characters** (elves and others on the aging chart, `story-bible/ages-and-lifespans.md`) never take part in romantic or sexual content before stated age 18, and their youth is never eroticized, including through descriptions of an adult-looking body.
- **Fairies are conjured as adults** and have no age; they are ancient, angelic minds. They are always written as unmistakably adult.
- **Newborns, offspring, and half-born children are never sexualized.** Reproduction scenes (an elf's birth through sunlight and love, a fairy immersing herself in her element to conjure another) depict only the adult parent. Violence against children is never framed as erotic.
- **Schools are adult.** Would-be witches are recruited only after they turn 18, so every student is an adult. Any school setting, including a spin-off, follows this.
- A "chosen" or frozen youthful appearance (a witch's forbidden aging magic, a vampire turned young) must still read as clearly adult.
- Any agent that sees a possible violation stops and raises it with the lead. The story-bible keeper treats it as a BLOCKER.

## 2. Women lead, men support

- Every story's protagonist is a woman. Her goals, choices, and growth drive the plot, and **her choice resolves the climax**.
- Male characters have real inner lives, but their arcs serve, complicate, or deepen hers. No man rescues her from her own story.

## 3. The two gazes are crossed

- Women are written through the **male gaze**, to draw men in and show women what they truly desire, without ever losing agency or interiority.
- Men are written through the **female gaze**, to draw young women in and show men what women truly want.
- Details and checklists: `gaze-guide.md`, and the agents `women-characters` and `men-characters`.

## 4. No moral framing

- **No character or faction is narrated as objectively good or evil.** Judgment comes only from the POV in the scene, and should read as her read, not as fact.
- Absent a POV filter, present competing philosophies, not verdicts. The light and dark elves are "neither right nor wrong." The death god's purpose is coherent on its own terms; the story doesn't have to agree with it, but it must not stack the deck.
- Let actions and consequences do the work.

## 5. The chain of intentions holds

`story-bible/chain-of-intentions.md` is the causal spine: who pushes human progress, who pulls toward equilibrium, and the two views of how balance is restored (swaying the living versus death). A story may complicate it from inside a POV, but must not contradict it.

## 6. Era discipline

- Species, populations, alliances, and who knows what are era-locked. Check `story-bible/timeline.md` and the era file before writing.
- Examples of facts that must never be borrowed early or late: fire, water, and air fairies are extinct after Era 6; the first witch never learns of the death god's portal; male elves appear only with the epilogue's half-births; there are no succubi anywhere in this world.

## 7. Telling order

The books are told out of chronological order (`story-bible/timeline.md`). A story must not reveal something the telling order holds back for a later book.

## 8. Magic is decided, not assumed

The guidelines in `writing-guide/magic-system/` describe what magic *can* do. A magic rule becomes canon only in `story-bible/magic/`, after it is rationalized and the author confirms it. A story that needs an unsettled rule flags it as proposed canon.
