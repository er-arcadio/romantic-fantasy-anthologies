# Project Plan: Fantasy Creature World & Story Repo

**Audience for this document:** an AI agent (e.g. Claude Code) that will create and organize a GitHub repository for a shared fantasy world and the stories set in it.

**Goal:** build a prehistoric-Earth world (Pangea, before the ice ages) populated by iconic fantasy archetypes, then write adult stories in it where **women are the protagonists and men support their journeys**, flipping the common trope of male protagonists with women in supporting roles. The stories pressure-test a central thesis about how to live (Section 3).

---

## 1. Core Principles

1. **Women lead.** Every story's protagonist is a woman. Her goals, choices, and growth drive the plot.
2. **Men support.** Male characters have real inner lives, but their arcs serve, complicate, or deepen the protagonist's journey. They are never the center of the story.
3. **Two gazes, deliberately crossed.**
   - **Men are written through the female gaze.** Purpose: pull young women into the story and show men what women truly want.
   - **Women are written through the male gaze.** Purpose: attract men to the story and show women what they truly desire.
   - Two dedicated agents enforce this (Section 8). The women remain protagonists with agency, so their allure is something they own and use, not something that sidelines them.
4. **Archetype filter.** Every creature must have (a) a strong, recognizable visual identity that artists have long depicted, and (b) a clear driving mission or motive in its lore. Pure cautionary-tale spirits (La Llorona, soucouyant, etc.) were cut for failing this test.
5. **No direct crossover between the lists.** The men's roster does not mirror a women's entry, so each character stands alone. (Vampire is the one deliberate shared archetype.)
6. **A few drive the plot; most seek peace and love.** Good and evil are driven by a select few who create havoc or stand against it. That moves the story at a high level. The vast majority of the world's people just search for peace, love, and sex, and the texture of everyday life should show that.
7. **Adults only in adult content.** See the hard rule in Section 5.

### Key distinctions to preserve in the lore
- **Demon vs fallen angel vs reaper:** the demon is *evil by nature* and wants power; the fallen angel is *good that fell* and wants redemption or freedom; the reaper is *neutral by nature* and bound by duty.
- **Core fantasy shared by the men:** a powerful being who is dangerous to everyone else but changes for her. What differs is what he changes *from*: ruthlessness (demon), guilt (fallen angel), detachment (reaper).
- **Elf vs dark elf:** same race, opposite society (light/shadow, mirroring Seelie/Unseelie).

## 3. Answered Questions (Source of Truth)

The AI should copy this section into `world/answered-questions.md` and treat it as settled unless the human changes it.

### A. Central thesis and commentary
> **"The meaning of life is purpose, community, and good health. Everything else, you will find, doesn't matter."**

The story's job is to **pressure-test** this claim, not simply endorse it. Each story should put it under strain from at least one of these angles:
- **Political structures** (courts, councils, governments, who gets to rule magic)
- **Religious structures** (heaven, hell, fae courts, the reaper's order, doctrine and dogma)
- **Tradition** ("because it's always been that way")

The thesis can bend, narrow, or be partially contradicted by what happens. That is the point.


### B. Conflict model
("Man" here means *person*, not male specifically.)
- **Person vs self:** drive and self tend to win, but not always in a tragic way. Self-victory can be liberating, costly, or bittersweet, and should not default to doom.
- **Person vs society:** the person tends to win over society.

### C. Common themes (in rough priority)
1. **Sex** (a central theme, written as an adult subject, not a garnish)
2. **Philosophy of living**, deliberately counter to mainstream Western norms
3. **Morals and moral compass** (what a compass is, where it comes from, when it fails)
4. **Mental health** (the self-conflict thread; treated honestly, not as a plot device)

### D. Setting
- **Prehistoric Earth, in the age of Pangea, before an ice age and before the one before that.** Everything happens on Earth. Some eras span only a few hundred years. "Modern" in any era means the present of that era's own timeline, not today.
- **There is no other planet.** Humans already live on Earth alongside angels, so nobody is recruited or transported.
- **The stories are not mainly set in a school.** In Era 7, **secret schools for witches** emerge as part of the story, and a school-focused spin-off is still possible. **Would-be witches are recruited only after they turn 18**, so the schools are adult academies, consistent with Section 5.

### E. Ages and appearance
- **Distribution:** apparent ages follow a bell curve with a **standard deviation of 3 years**. Women center on about **24** and men on about **34**, so most women look over 21. A smaller chunk looks 18 to 21 (still adult-looking but notably younger), and a very small tail is the rapidly-maturing children.
- **Women:** most look young, since most of these creatures are immortal. Witches who learn the forbidden magic freeze at an apparent age that reflects what men tend to prefer (a commentary hook worth exploring). **Hags are the exception** and show age.
- **Men:** in-world rationale for the later center: **men need more time to develop and learn to harness their power and skill.**
- **Immortals stay put** at their chosen or frozen age. **Mortals age slowly.**
- **Children** exist in both cases and age to maturity rapidly. **Women's aging schedule** (apparent age against stated age):

| Apparent age | Time it takes | Stated age on arrival |
|---|---|---|
| Birth to 18 ("maturity") | 18 months | 1.5 years |
| 18 to 21 | 15 years | 16.5 years |
| 21 to 24 | 150 years | 166.5 years |
| 24 to 27 | 150 years | 316.5 years |
| 27 (immortals stop here) | none | none |
| 27 to 30 (mortals only; "old") | 15 years | 331.5 years |
| 30 to 33 (final year) | 1 year | 332.5 years |

- **Men** follow the same schedule, except it takes **36 months (3 years)** to reach maturity, which for men is apparent age **28**. How the later bands run for men is not yet specified (Section 6D).
- **Who uses the chart:** it applies to everyone **except humans, witches, fairies, and vampires** (so elves, dark elves, drow, werewolves, demons, and the rest). The exceptions:
  - **Humans** age regularly.
  - **Witches** age regularly, like humans, until they learn the **forbidden magic** that stops aging. They then freeze at an apparent age of **21 to 27**, depending on skill.
  - **Hags** are witches who never use the forbidden magic (they reject it on principle, never learn it, or learn it poorly), so they keep aging and show it.
  - **Vampires** start human and are turned by another vampire between apparent ages **18 and 30**. They then stay at that age forever.
  - **Fairies** are **conjured, never born**, always adult, and **have no age at all**: they are essentially gods or angels. Each is an ancient mind (a clone of the fairy who conjured her, ultimately of a former angel) in a clearly adult form. They do not use the chart. The original fairies were angels for ages before they took the form (see 4B).
  - **Elves** use the chart in full, including the rapid childhood: they are the maturity-period species.
- **Ages are written as plain stated years.** A woman who looks 18 would say she is 2 years old. The apparent-age equivalence exists only to give the reader a sense of maturity and physical presence.
- Section 5 decides which characters can appear in sexual or romantic content, and it goes by **stated years**. Remaining nuances to iron out are in Section 6D.

### 4A-2. The chain of intentions (the engine of the plot)
This is the causal spine of the whole world. Keep every era consistent with it.
1. **Humans are progressing, and the gods supervise.**
2. **First generation (fairies):** come to push toward more progress, with hints. Not enough.
3. **Second generation (elves):** come to pull toward even more progress. Some elves pull against it, but not strongly enough.
4. **The fallen angels** come to restore equilibrium by pulling toward **less progress**, meaning humans' vices and hedonism. This is justified because humans are over-fixed on progress.
5. **Two views of how to restore balance:**
   - **The male fallen angel (the future death god)** has **always believed in neutrality**, even as an angel. His flaw is the method: he **deludes himself that killing all magical beings is the only way to true neutrality**. He does not learn otherwise; he only changes tactics.
   - **The female fallen angel (the leader)** wants **balance by swaying the living**, a little more left and a little more right, **never by killing**.
6. **The witches** share the leader's goal of balance without killing, **except the evil witch**, who sides with the death god.
7. **Containment, then the demons:** the first witch contains the death god in the underworld, using the power of the two women fallen angels. She then builds the coven and **uses the contained death god to make the demons**, male beings meant to **seduce the progressive elves**. A coven sister, seduced by the death god, goes along to help, secretly intending to win his heart and join his cause. She becomes the **evil witch**.
8. **The demons**, built to seduce the progressive elves, end up seducing the **dark elves**, **plenty of other witches**, and **the fairies** too.
9. **The vampires:** after the evil witch is defeated, the death god decides his methods must be **more infectious and sly**. The undead vampire is his first move, aimed at killing **witches and elves**, though vampires also kill and recruit **humans**. They are created for **sheer killing**, to wipe out as many beings as possible. They are strong enough to kill witches, elves, and some demons, but **not strong enough to kill fairies in their full, most powerful form**. **Fairy vulnerability (decided):** in human form a fairy has no magic, so it is the **one form in which vampires can kill her**. Integrating with humans is exactly what puts fairies at risk.
10. **Underlying theme:** extremes versus the middle path. The story keeps testing whether balance is reached through drastic action or by small adjustments.

### 4B. Era 1: Fairies, elves, and humans on Pangea (oldest era)
**Rewritten.** Humans already live on Earth, and among them are godlike beings who seem brought by divinity.
- **Structure and protagonists (decided):** Era 1 is **told in two parts, first generation then second generation**. **Part one:** a first-generation fairy leads the march of the original angels as the group's leader. **Part two:** two elves lead the march of the second generation. All three are main protagonists. (My reading: the two elves are the ones whose beliefs split the elves into light and dark, since that split ends the second generation. Confirm.) How the two parts braid with Era 4 is still open.
- **The original angels (first generation):** a group of **women angels** told God, ruler of the heavens, that they would go down as **fairies** to guide humans through their most trying times. They were unified on that mission and **sacrificed their lives (their place among the angels) to it, and are bound by it.** Fairies only give hints: they appear to humans as fairies as a signal ("come this way"), or blend in as humans at times without joining society, and **they never speak to humans**, so as not to tell them outright which path to take. They are always women because a woman's appearance eases nerves in desperate times.
- **First-generation fairy forms:** the original fairies can present in **three forms**: (1) a **small fairy form** for quick flying and giving hints (no speaking); (2) a **human form** for integration and understanding, **which can speak, shows freckles so the audience knows she is a fairy, and has no magic**; (3) a **full form**, a full-size fairy with human height and wings, **the most powerful in magic**. (Open: whether conjured fairies have all three forms.)
- **The second generation, the elves:** when hints were not enough, a second group of **women angels**, inspired by the first, came down as **elves** with more integration in mind. They did not hide being elves and openly helped society progress. They were so helpful that they **advanced human technology quickly, in a way that was not as close to free will as God wanted**. Elves are never called "angels from heaven," though it is easy to read them that way.
- **God's response:** God and most angels judged that this is simply the natural course and let things be. **Four angels, very outspoken in disagreement, went against God's wishes and the other angels and were outcast into the human world. These are the fallen angels (Era 2).**
- **The elf split came before the fallen angels:** the elves themselves began to realize they were helping too much, in a biased direction. Two thoughts emerged, creating **elves and dark elves**. The **elves push toward human progress**, and the **dark elves counter by pulling humans toward temptations and vices**. The elves have a **light aesthetic** and the dark elves a **darker aesthetic**. This split is about belief and look only; neither is part demon. Neither side is right or wrong; they hold two opposing viewpoints.
- **Fairy elements and roles:** fire, water, air (wind), earth (forest), and soul/spirit. **Soul fairies support humans in times of birth and death.** They rarely conjure more of their kind, out of shame at having to immerse themselves in death or birth, so they are **endangered from early on**. When vampires, drow, and demons arrive, some soul fairies who are especially drawn to death and birth become exceptions (mainly with vampires), and some are **seduced by the death god**.
- **Fairies and elves never crossed with other races at this point.**
- **Reproduction rules:**
  - **Fairies** are **conjured, not born**. A fairy conjures another by **immersing herself in the element she came from or dedicated herself to** (earth, wind, fire, water, or soul/spirit; spirit stays as the fifth element). Each new fairy is **an adult from the start** (no childhood or adolescence) and a **mental clone of the conjuring fairy** with a different appearance (skin, hair, eye color). Fairies can have sex with other beings, **but can never conceive or produce a child with another being**: the parts exist but do not work for reproduction.
  - **Elves** are more human-like. They use the **maturity period in Section 3E** and reproduce by **parthenogenesis with sunlight and love**, usually with another elf (often sexually but not biologically). Later they can also reproduce with a human or another creature.
- **No male elves or fairies exist**, and the reason is biological: parthenogenesis produces only women, so a male elf requires a human father. This is why male elves appear only with the half-births in the epilogue.
- **Why elf reproduction with others began:** elves and fairies came with no intention of reproducing with humans. It was never in their thinking, though humans were seduced by them, and there were a few taboo romances. When the fallen angels arrived they brought that idea to life, which raised sexual tension and disrupted the norm. At the climax of everyone trying to keep order, the **elves** were forced to reproduce with others to keep the balance. (Fairies could not, see above. When and why elves stopped reproducing among themselves is still open.)
- Told in the braided 3rd telling, alongside Era 4.

### 4C. Era 2: The origin (told 2nd)
0. **Protagonists (decided):** two main women whose stories collide. **(1) The leader** of the fallen angels, and **(2) the witch who inherits her powers**, who is given a human background and is connected to the leader later. The **evil witch** is the antagonist and ties to the first (inheriting) witch: her turn to aggressive evil should come as a **surprise**, and she sides with the death god's plan of balance through death. (Open: her tie and backlash against the first witch, see open items.) The **second angel woman** has her own story but is a **support** role. The death god and the other angel man support and do not lead.
1. **The four fallen angels (2 men, 2 women)** are the outcasts of Section 4B. Their plan: **end the fairies' and elves' involvement in human progress and keep the groups separate.** The movement is **led by the woman (the leader)** and supported by the other woman and the two men. They come to live among humans on Earth and **do not hide**: they want their presence and cause known.
2. One of the fallen angel men believes they must be **more powerful to truly restore balance**. He convinces the other two to help kill the leader.
3. He **consumes the leader's spiritual energy (her soul)**, but does not find the power in her, because she has already passed it on. **He then consumes the other angel man right after, and gets what he wanted.** He becomes the **death god / reaper**.
4. **The leader creates the first witch**, passing her power to her. The **second angel woman helps create an avenue for more witches to be created**, sacrificing herself in the process. **The first witch starts the first coven.** (Witches do not already exist on Earth; the fallen angels create them.)
5. **The containment battle:** the first witch **contains the death god in the underworld**. It takes a great deal out of her, and she does it by drawing on the power of **the two women fallen angels** (the leader and the second woman). (Where the underworld is, perhaps another dimension, is open. He is trapped there, and acts on the world through the evil witch until she opens a secret portal; see item 8.)
6. **The coven and the demons:** in the aftermath, the first witch **builds the coven**. She realizes she can **use the contained death god to make the demons**: **male** beings meant to **seduce the progressive elves** who pull humans toward more progress. (They are male because some good people can be infected by evil, and because men are physically stronger than women.) **Demon curse:** when a demon has a child with a **mortal**, the child must be **male**, so the seduction continues. **Another coven member goes to help**. She has been seduced by the death god, and her **secret intention is to win his heart and join his cause**. She becomes the **evil witch**.
7. **What the demons actually did:** built for the progressive elves, they ended up seducing the **dark elves**, **plenty of other witches**, and **the fairies**. Seducing elves also produced the **drow**: half demon, half elf, with distinct purple skin, a people separate from the dark elves though they share some dark aesthetic and the pull toward hedonism. **Drow can be male or female**: females lean closer to elves with slight demon elements, and males lean more demon with slight elf elements.
8. **The era ends:** the **evil witch** takes the lead antagonist role, **spreading death across the Earth with the death god's support** from the underworld. The death god **acts through her**, since he is trapped. **She opens a secret portal** that gives him a way out. The **coven, helped by the (light) elves, defeats her**, and **he does not come through the portal to save her**. He **learns nothing about his goal**, only that his methods must be **more infectious and sly**: *"The women before me infected this world little by little over time. Change is slow and unnoticed. I see that now."*
   - **The portal afterwards:** for the same reason, he never uses it for big attacks. He uses it to **visit people**: those **near death**, to find the perfect candidate for Dracula; **witches and dark elves**, to seduce them; and he is **accidentally seduced** himself by **witches and some fire fairies**. Later, **female vampires** stop by to see him. **Secrecy:** no one knows about the portal except the people he visits. Some share his cause and happily keep his secret; others are infatuated with him and would never tell for fear of losing the connection. **The first witch never finds out.**
9. **The male fallen angels (later):** the two generations of women "missionary" angels (fairies, then elves) left many male angels frustrated and bored. After the first four, **more male angels trickle in every now and then**, kicked out to live among the rest. (Mostly left unresolved on purpose.) They scare the demons off their seduction of the elves and fairies and want their women back **emotionally**: "that's our women, let's go get them." Sex happens out of desire and a feeling of home and instinct. It is sexy at times and tragic at others, because fairies never conceive.

### 4D. Era 3: The era of the vampires (told 1st)
**Protagonists (decided):** three women in three separate stories that come together.
- **A witch** who fights for what is right despite the prejudice against witches, because she is in love with a human who was turned into a vampire.
- **A human woman** turned into a vampire who likes what is happening, and who is the witch's beloved. **Confirmed:** theirs is a love between two women, taboo at first, then a friendship and partnership with a shared purpose.
- **A fae** with a side story: a woman looking for justice and truth.
- The death god supports their journeys and does not lead any of them.

*The arrival has been rewritten (no planet, no recruitment).*

This era takes place in the world after the Era 2 conflict has resolved, but it is the first one the reader sees.
- The demons unite to create a **demon lord**.
- **Rewritten:** the death god no longer recruits humans, who already live on Earth. After the evil witch's defeat he decides his methods must be more infectious and sly. He sends the soul of a dead human back as **"Dracula,"** the original vampire, which fits his domain and echoes the Dracula legend. The vampires are made with the intention of **sheer killing**, aimed first at **witches and elves**, though they also kill and recruit **humans**. They are strong enough to kill witches, elves, and some demons, but not fairies in their full form (only in their magic-less human form). The seed is planted.
- The witches are met with prejudice after the war. **Elves**, pushed by the upheaval, reproduce with others (Section 4B). (Fairies cannot.) Both groups initially blame the witches for the sudden **vampires**, and the prejudice is partly justified by the evil witch and the demons.
- The fae hear of humans turning into vampires and suspect it is not the witches' doing. The witches deny involvement and unite against the demons, the demon lord, and the vampires.
- **Equilibrium is restored, with cross-species romances everywhere.** But the witches, vampires, and demons blend so well with humans that tension builds amid the apparent peace.

### 4F. Era 5: The great demon massacre and the rise of the werewolves (told 4th)
**Known in-world as the Great Demon Massacre, and the rise of the werewolves.**

**Protagonists (decided):** the **witch** and the **elf** who make the first werewolf.
- **The witch** rejects forcing equality if it means being evil (a stand against the death god's "balance through death").
- **The elf** wants to make a stand against the **dark elves who bullied her**.
- These motives sit in their **subconscious** when they desire and design their third. They **emerge once they see his potential**.

**Events:**
1. **Origin:** the witch and the elf want **a man they can share**. In the woods, they turn a **human man** into the first werewolf (in a three-way). It is sexy first, then it gets out of control.
2. **First incident:** during the curse's first episode, he **kills a demon**. Rumors spread of what was seen.
3. **Weaponizing him:** the witch and the elf **make him stronger** and try to convince him to **kill vampires** too.
4. **Losing control:** his strength grows too great, and **he is not himself in wolf form**. He grows sloppy and **bites and infects a few other human men**.
5. **The other werewolves differ:** those who follow the first are **more level-headed in wolf form**. They are not fully present, but they act on **instinct and subconscious**, not pure rage.
6. **The massacre:** the first werewolf goes fully out of control and **starts killing demons** en masse.
7. **Dracula intervenes:** he does not like the demons being killed, but **he is no match** for the first werewolf and **retreats with his vampires**.
8. **The witches hunt for the man in human form but cannot find him.**
9. **He kills the demon lord**, then turns on **humans**, killing and converting them. The other werewolves try to stop him but are **too afraid**.
10. **The fairies unite** and finally **put an end to him**. **The fairy leaders of each element strike the final blow together.**
11. **The remaining few demons retreat to the underworld** with the death god.

**The death god's stance:** he is **a little ashamed of the demons** he made with the first witch. They were designed to bring equality, but he still believes death is the only way to true equality. So **he does not intervene**: the werewolf is, in a way, serving his cause. He also hopes the massacre will make people see that **there will never be peace while good and evil are locked in a tug of war**.

**Rules:** werewolves never turn women. Weakness: **beautiful women**.

**Earlier outline notes:** the elves are attracted to the wolves and come out of hiding; a good witch and a bad witch hook up and visit the death god for advice (and something more). Placement of these two is open. **The succubus is dropped from the world.**

### 4F-2. Era 6: The Civil War (fairies vs elves)
**Protagonists (decided):** **the elf from Era 5** (who co-created the first werewolf) and **a fairy who helped take down the first werewolf**. They stand on opposite sides of the war.

**The conflict:**
- **Fairies:** side with humans. They watched humans be converted into werewolves and die in the massacre. To them, the werewolves, and death itself, were wrong. They are angry and need to take it out. **They want the remaining werewolves gone.**
- **Elves:** see the massacre as an overall improvement. All the demons are gone, and the humans were a small price to pay for a general direction of good. They believe werewolves are good in the long run, and that the remaining wolves are on the humans' side at a subconscious level, **so they should be kept**.
- **Witches:** appreciate the intent on both sides but **do not like the killing**. They fight for peace, but are **too weak to compete** with the elves or fairies.
- **Vampires:** do not care that the magical creatures fight each other. They just keep feeding on mortals to continue their race.
- **The death god and the demons (now living with him):** approve, because it means more death. They hold back and watch.

**How the war goes:**
1. **Fire fairies** lead the charge and take the heaviest losses.
2. **Water fairies** take a hard hit and become endangered.
3. **Earth (forest) fairies** retreat to the forest before it gets too bad.
4. **Soul fairies** stay out of it from the start, since they are already endangered.
5. **Near the end, the elves are winning** (but have lost many troops), and the fairies are losing badly.
6. **The drow, outcast by the elves**, join the death god and the demons, occasionally traveling between Earth and the underworld.
7. **The cursed hags:** the death god and the drow plot to **infect the minds of the few hags** on the land. The hags are **cursed with a hunger that forces them to feed on fairies to survive**. (No succubi are made.)
8. The cursed hags **secretly aid the elves** by consuming any fairies they can catch: many water fairies, many air fairies, and some soul fairies. They also consume some elves in the process.
9. **Outcome:** **fire, water, and air fairies go extinct.** Many earth (forest) fairies remain, and only a few soul fairies survive.

**Aftermath:**
- **Elves:** lose many in the battle.   - **Integrated and disguised among humans**, protecting them from vampires and werewolves while **trying not to be seduced**.

**The hags' end:** in the final stretch of the witches' era, **the witches protecting the fairies defeat the cursed hags**.

**How the saga ends (decided):** a blend of an **uneasy balance** and the death god **quietly winning** underneath it. No side wins outright. Balance is an ongoing practice, while the death god's slow influence keeps shaping the world from below.

**The thesis verdict, group by group** (purpose, community, health):
- **Witches:** find all three. *Purpose:* protecting fairies in the forest and humans in the cities. *Community:* the covens and the secret schools.
- **Forest fairies:** find all three. *Purpose:* guiding humans near nature. *Community:* each other in the woods.
- **Soul fairies:** find all three. *Purpose:* tending birth and death, and quietly protecting humans. *Community:* the humans they live among. Their health stays fragile, since their human form has no magic.
- **The death god:** finds all three, which is uncomfortable for the reader on purpose. *Purpose:* the strongest of anyone's since his origin, ultimate balance through death. *Community:* he cares for the demons and drow and gives them a shared purpose.

- **Werewolves: a tragedy.** They have community and health but **no purpose**, held back by shame at being the root of the massacre.
- **Fallen angels: a tragedy.** They have purpose (wanting their women back) but **no community**: few, scattered, and still seeking.
- **Elves (light and dark): open.** They have community and safety in isolation, but their purpose is mostly to stay out of the way. Undecided whether that counts as the thesis working or as a hollow version of it.

**Writing note:** the werewolves' and fallen angels' missing pieces are deliberate. Their tragedy should be **juxtaposed against the groups who found peace**. The pattern: those who found peace largely did so by separating, while those still missing something either caused the most harm or could not let go.

## 5. Hard Rule: Adult Content and Age

This overrides every other instruction in the repo, including style guides, agents, and the era outlines.

- **All characters in sexual or romantic content must be unambiguously adult**: adult in apparent age, behavior, mind, and development. A character who is "technically old" but a child in appearance, mind, or development does not qualify.
- **Stated age governs, not apparent age.** Ages are written in plain stated years (Section 3E). **A character may appear in sexual or romantic content only if their stated age is 18 or older**, and the writing must also make adulthood obvious (adult features, voice, interior life, history, and agency). Apparent age never overrides this: a character who looks 18 but is stated as 2 years old is a child in the text and is never part of such content.
- **Consequence for the age model:** for species on the chart (elves, dark elves, drow, and so on), women in the apparent 18 to 21 band (stated ages 1.5 to 16.5) are never in sexual or romantic content, and they first become eligible at stated age 18, an apparent age just over 21. **Fairies** have no age and are ancient, godlike minds in clearly adult forms, so the stated-age test does not apply to them. They must still always be written as unmistakably adult. For humans, witches, and vampires, stated age equals years lived, so a clearly adult character of 18 or older is eligible. The same stated-age rule applies to men, whatever their apparent age. Rapidly-maturing characters can still be rich, important, non-sexual characters (friends, rivals, family, mentors), and their growth can be part of the story.
- **Rapidly-maturing children never take part in romantic or sexual content** during childhood or adolescence, and their youth is never eroticized, including through descriptions of an adult-looking body. They become eligible only when their stated age reaches 18 and the text shows full adult maturity.
- **Newborns and offspring are never sexualized.** Asexual reproduction scenes (elf light-and-love births, fairies conjuring more fairies) depict the adult parent only. The offspring who result are never part of sexual content.
- **Half-born children (epilogue) are never sexualized.** Violence against them (such as vampires feeding on them) is never framed as erotic or arousing.
- **No school setting or spin-off may include sexual or romantic content between students unless every one of them is clearly an adult.** A school spin-off must be an adult academy or apprenticeship, not a high-school analog.
- "Chosen" youthful appearance (witch glamour, immortal frozen age) must still read as clearly adult.
- Both agents (Section 8) must flag and stop any draft that violates this.

## 8. The Two Agents

Create these as agent definition files in `.claude/agents/`. Each applies a different gaze on purpose.

### Agent 1: `women-characters` (male gaze)
**Purpose:** write and review the female characters (witch, hag, fae, elf, dark elf, vampire, kitsune) through the male gaze. The goal is to attract men to the story and show women what they truly desire.

What this means in practice:
- **Visual allure and aesthetic are foregrounded.** Her archetype imagery is vivid, sensual, and iconic, rendered the way an attentive male observer would perceive it.
- **Allure is power, not decoration.** She knows she is looked at and uses it. Her mission (see roster) still drives the plot, and she makes the decisions.
- **What women truly desire:** the story shows the woman as she wants to be seen and what she wants for herself (freedom, mastery, being wanted on her own terms), so the gaze serves her.
- **Hags** subvert the default: the gaze meets age and refuses to look away or soften, which is part of the commentary.
- **Apparent age** follows the bell curve around 24 (standard deviation 3), written in plain stated years. The Section 5 stated-age rule governs what can appear in sexual or romantic content.
- **She is never reduced to the gaze.** Interiority, goals, and wit remain on the page.

Review checklist: Is her allure vivid and archetype-specific? Does she drive the scene and use her allure deliberately? Is her mission visible? Does the age rule in Section 5 hold?

### Agent 2: `men-characters` (female gaze)
**Purpose:** write and review the male characters (vampire, werewolf, demon, fallen angel, reaper) through the female gaze. The goal is to pull young women into the story and show men what women truly want.

What this means in practice:
- **Seen through her eyes.** Describe him through what she notices: hands, voice, attentiveness, stillness, how he treats others, how he listens.
- **Desire is built on being understood.** Attractiveness comes from emotional attention, competence, restraint, devotion, and respect, not just physique.
- **The core fantasy:** he is dangerous to everyone else but changes for her. Use the appeal types in the roster.
- **Apparent age** follows the bell curve around 34 (standard deviation 3), reflecting that men need more time to develop and harness their power and skill. Ages are written in plain stated years, and the Section 5 stated-age rule governs what can appear in sexual or romantic content.
- **Support, not center.** He deepens, challenges, protects, or tests her. His arc resolves in relation to hers, and he does not rescue her from her own story.
- **She keeps her power.** Dominance and danger are fantasy flavors, but he never overrides her choices.

Review checklist: Is he reacting to her decisions or making them for her? Does he support her goal? Is his appeal tied to his specific archetype and mission? Does the age rule in Section 5 hold?
